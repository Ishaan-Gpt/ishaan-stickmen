# The rulebook: what the What If hits share (measured 2026-11-03)

We took the first 90 s of seven of the genre's most viewed films and measured them from contact sheets (one frame a second) and
word-timed transcripts.

| Channel, film views | Medium | Title lands | Number on screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ifverse, 4.6M | 2D with depth: sunset, rim light, blur | 0:35 | a stopwatch, 0.25 s against 0.025 s |
| Minute Zero, 2.2M | low-poly 3D, first person | 0:06 | speed climbing on a HUD |
| Ifinite, 1.7M | 2D with depth: rainy city, glowing rims | 0:32 | a timer over every head |

## Script
1. Second person, present tense, calm. The top film reads at about 1.6 words a second, with silence between lines.
2. Line one is an everyday moment with the power already working, or a number that contradicts the words. The title comes after
   the proof, at about 0:30 (the median).
3. Another character says one short line of disbelief. That line is the viewer's stand-in.
4. One number contrast, said and shown: normal against you.
5. After the title: how it works in plain words, then a spine of days or rules, then the cost.

## Look
6. The top character films are 2D that feels 3D. The real-3D character channels sit lower (about 900K).
7. Matte black ball heads, with a face made of eyes and a mouth line only. One colour per character. A rim of light on every silhouette.
8. Depth from planes: a blurred foreground, a sharp subject, a soft background with glowing windows. One light mood per film.
9. The number is a designed object over the head, coloured by meaning, and it changes on the beat.
10. A new shot every 1-2 s, often two angles of one moment. Science is shown as a simple labelled diagram.

## The market
Uploads in the genre went from 11 in its first week to 147 in its third, while the median new film fell from about 247K views to 21K.
Straight copies die. A fresh premise in the same shape can still break out from a channel with a few thousand subscribers.
